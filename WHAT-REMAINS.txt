Project goal:
Build a publication-quality research platform for systematically comparing existing and newly proposed audio/DSP transformations according to:

1. Human speech intelligibility.
2. Human audio-CAPTCHA task success.
3. Automatic speech-recognition robustness.
4. ASR transcription degradation.
5. Robustness against preprocessing and defense pipelines.
6. Perceptual audio quality.
7. Psychoacoustic plausibility.
8. Computational cost.
9. Cross-model transferability.
10. Accessibility and usability.
11. Statistical reliability and reproducibility.

The final platform must support a complete Human-vs-ASR research study and produce paper-ready tables, plots, rankings, statistical summaries, experiment manifests, and reproducible reports.

Do not treat this as a simple collection of audio effects. The central research question is:

“Which controlled audio transformations produce the largest statistically reliable gap between human speech understanding and automated speech-recognition performance while preserving acceptable perceptual quality, accessibility, and practical usability?”

The project must not claim that a transformation is secure merely because one ASR model fails. All conclusions must be based on multiple ASR models, multiple preprocessing conditions, held-out speakers, real speech datasets, human evaluation, and uncertainty-aware statistical analysis.
