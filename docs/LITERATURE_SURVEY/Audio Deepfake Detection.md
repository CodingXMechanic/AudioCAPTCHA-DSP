# Audio Deepfake Detection: A Survey

## Full reference

## One-line contribution
A comprehensive survey that taxonomizes deepfake audio types, competitions, datasets, features, classifiers, end-to-end models and generalization methods for audio deepfake detection, and — unlike prior reviews — performs a unified re-evaluation of representative features, classifiers and end-to-end models on ASVspoof 2021 DF, ADD 2023 FG-D and the out-of-domain In-the-Wild set, showing consistent EER degradation of 2%–52% out of domain.

## Problem & motivation
Prior surveys focus on spoofing countermeasures protecting automatic speaker verification (ASV) systems (Wu et al. 2015, ASVspoof-centric reviews 2020–2021) rather than on protecting humans from being deceived by deepfake audio, and existing reviews lack a consistent experimental comparison across methods. This survey therefore (1) highlights key differences across deepfake audio types, (2) summarizes competitions, datasets, features, classifiers and evaluation, and (3) runs a unified comparison of representative features/classifiers on in-domain (ASVspoof 2021 DF, ADD 2023 FG-D) vs out-of-domain (In-the-Wild) test sets, since poor generalization to unseen attacks/conditions is the central unsolved problem. Motivating use case printed: fraudsters mimicked a CEO's voice to demand a fraudulent transfer of USD 243,000 in 2019.

## Method (precise but simple language, 5-12 lines; key equations if present)
1. Taxonomy of five deepfake audio types (Table 1): TTS (speaker identity + content, fully), voice conversion (identity, fully), emotion fake, scene fake, partially fake (content, partially) — all AI-aided.
2. Pipeline detectors = front-end feature extractor + back-end classifier; end-to-end detectors jointly optimize feature extraction and classification on raw waveform (Figure 1).
3. Feature taxonomy (Figure 3): short-term spectral (magnitude: LMS/RLMS, LPS/Cep/LFCC/MFCC/IMFCC/LPCC; phase: GD/MGD/MGDCC, IF/BPD/RPS/CosPhase), long-term spectral (STFT-based ModSpec/SDC/FDLP/LBP, CQT-based CQCC/eCQCC/CQTMGD, Hilbert-based MHEC, wavelet-based CFCC/CFCCIF/MWPC), prosodic (F0/pitch, energy, duration), and deep features (learnable spectral: learned FBCC/ConvRBM/nnAudio/FastAudio, TD-FBanks/SincNet/LEAF; supervised embeddings: spoof/emotion/speaker/pronunciation; self-supervised embeddings: Wav2vec/Wav2vec2.0, XLS-R, HuBERT).
4. Classifier taxonomy (Table 4): traditional (SVM, GMM) vs deep (CNN/LCNN, ResNet/AFN, Res2Net, SENet/ASSERT, GNN/GAT, DARTS/PC-DARTS) vs end-to-end (CRNNSpoof, RawNet2/TO-RawNet, RawGAT-ST/AASIST, Raw PC-DARTS, Rawformer/SE-Rawformer), each with printed pros/cons.
5. Generalization methods reviewed: loss-function approaches (LMCL + online frequency masking, one-class learning, SAMO speaker-attractor multi-center) and continual learning (DFWF, RAWM) against catastrophic forgetting.
6. Unified experiments: features (MFCC, LPS, LFCC, IMFCC, CQCC, F0 via YAPPT with 25 ms window/10 ms shift, energy 25 ms/10 ms, HuBERT-based duration, XLS-R 1024-d vectors per 10 ms segment, and concatenations A+B+C+LFCC / A+B+C+XLS-R) × classifiers (512-component GMM; LCNN with LSTM + average pooling; ASSERT; ResNet-34; Res2Net, AFN, GRU, GAT from open source) trained with Adam, lr 5×10⁻⁵, 200 epochs, batch size 32, on ASVspoof 2021 DF or ADD 2023 FG-D training sets and evaluated in-domain plus In-the-Wild; end-to-end RawNet2 and AASIST trained identically.
7. Metric: threshold-free EER — P_fa(θ) = #{fake trials with score > θ}/#{total fake trials}, P_miss(θ) = #{genuine trials < θ}/#{total genuine trials}, EER = P_fa(θ_EER) = P_miss(θ_EER); ADD uses weighted EER WEER = α·EER_R1 + β·EER_R2 over two evaluation rounds.

## Datasets / corpora used
Survey-level: no new corpus is collected. Datasets reviewed and tabulated (Table 3): SAS, PhoneSpoof, ASVspoof 2015/2017/2019/2021 (LA/PA/DF), FoR, WaveFake, HAD (source of ADD 2022 PF), FMFCC-A, EmoFake, SceneFake, In-the-Wild (2022, English celebrities/politicians from social networks and video platforms), ADD 2022 (LF, PF, FG) and ADD 2023 (FG, RL, AR). Unified experiments use ASVspoof 2021 DF (train/test), ADD 2023 FG-D (train/test), and In-the-Wild (test only, out-of-domain).

## Models / systems evaluated
- Features evaluated: MFCC, LPS, LFCC, IMFCC, CQCC, F0 (YAPPT), energy, duration (HuBERT-based), XLS-R (Wav2vec2.0 variant pretrained on 53 languages / 56 thousand hours, 1024-d per 10 ms), plus concatenations A+B+C+LFCC and A+B+C+XLS-R.
- Classifiers: 512-component GMM, LCNN (with LSTM + average pooling), ASSERT (SENet+ResNet), ResNet-34, Res2Net, AFN, GRU, GAT — trained Adam lr 5×10⁻⁵, 200 epochs, batch 32.
- End-to-end models: RawNet2, AASIST (open-source implementations, same training recipe).
- Competition systems summarized (not re-run): top-3 systems of ASVspoof 2015/2017/2019/2021 and ADD 2022/2023 with their features/classifiers (Table 5).

## Human-study details (if any: n participants, protocol, key numbers)
None — no human listening study is reported anywhere in the paper. The only human reference is prospective: future directions state that EER should be questioned as a metric and that "we should consider human detection capabilities, as well as the differences between humans and machines for detecting deepfake audio" — no participant counts, protocols or perceptual numbers are printed.

## Limitations acknowledged by authors
- Poor generalization: EER rises 2%–52% on out-of-domain tests; classifiers and end-to-end models degrade sharply under distribution/language mismatch (In-the-Wild is a different language from ADD training data).
- Lack of large-scale in-the-wild datasets: most datasets are simulated, single-language (mostly English or Chinese), not collected under realistic channel/noise conditions — risking language-dependent detectors.
- Rapidly evolving generators make detection a moving target; ADD's game format is "simple and lacks intelligence".
- Interpretability is lacking: binary real/fake output without localizing manipulated intervals or attributing the generating method; ADD 2023's localization/attribution sub-challenges are "still in infancy".
- Evaluation metrics may be unreasonable: EER is used everywhere but its validity for audio deepfake detection is unverified; human detection capability and human–machine differences are ignored.
- (Implicit) results are benchmark-bound: the unified comparison only covers two training sets and one out-of-domain test set, with a single training recipe.

## Relevance to our project (one specific paragraph)
This survey is the map of the machine-side threat model for AudioCAPTCHA-DSP: it tells us which artifacts and detectors exist (LFCC/CQCC/XLS-R features, GMM/LCNN/ResNet/AASIST classifiers) and, crucially, quantifies how badly they fail out of domain (In-the-Wild EER penalties of 2%–52%; e.g., AASIST 19.77→34.81, GAT+XLS-R 14.91→44.31). For our Human-ASR Gap framework this matters three ways: (1) our DSP distortion suite (noise, filtering, time-stretching, etc.) is exactly the "low-quality/out-of-distribution channel" regime the survey shows detectors and ASR models are brittle in, while human listeners are comparatively robust — so the paper's generalization tables are the strongest published justification for expecting a measurable HAG under distortion; (2) its closing call to "consider human detection capabilities, as well as the differences between humans and machines" and to question EER as a metric is a direct, citable motivation for our human-grounded evaluation instead of machine-only scores; (3) its dataset table (In-the-Wild only 38.0 h and collected from social platforms, most others simulated) documents the real-world data gap our perceptual, human-in-the-loop experiments address, and its feature/classifier taxonomy is the reference list if we later test whether our distortions also defeat spoof/deepfake detectors (e.g., XLS-R or AASIST) in addition to Whisper/Vosk.
